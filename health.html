Health Information ❤️

Detailed guide to common illnesses in Dogs, Cats, Rabbits, and Birds — including symptoms and what to do.

🐶 Common Illnesses in Dogs

1. Parvovirus (Parvo)

What it is: Highly contagious virus that attacks the intestines and immune system, especially dangerous in puppies.

Symptoms: Severe vomiting, bloody diarrhea, extreme lethargy, loss of appetite, fever, dehydration.

What to do: This is a medical emergency. Take the dog to a vet immediately. Vaccination is the best prevention.

2. Kennel Cough

What it is: Contagious respiratory infection.

Symptoms: Strong dry cough, gagging, runny nose, mild fever.

What to do: Usually mild but can become serious in puppies or senior dogs. See a vet if it lasts more than a few days.

3. Ear Infections

What it is: Very common, especially in dogs with floppy ears or allergies.

Symptoms: Head shaking, scratching ears, bad smell, redness, discharge.

What to do: Clean only with vet-approved solution. Visit the vet for proper treatment.

4. Hip Dysplasia

What it is: Genetic joint problem common in larger breeds.

Symptoms: Difficulty standing, limping, reluctance to jump or climb stairs.

What to do: Weight control, joint supplements, and veterinary care help manage it.

5. Skin Allergies & Hot Spots

What it is: Allergic reactions to food, fleas, or environment.

Symptoms: Intense itching, red skin, hair loss, licking paws, hot spots.

What to do: Identify and remove the cause. Vet may prescribe medication or special diet.

🐱 Common Illnesses in Cats

1. Urinary Blockage / FLUTD

What it is: Inflammation or blockage of the urinary tract. Life-threatening in male cats.

Symptoms: Straining in litter box, frequent attempts to urinate, crying, blood in urine.

What to do: Emergency if the cat cannot urinate. Take to vet immediately.

2. Upper Respiratory Infection (Cat Flu)

What it is: Viral or bacterial infection of nose, throat, and eyes.

Symptoms: Sneezing, runny nose, watery eyes, loss of appetite, mild fever.

What to do: Keep warm, encourage eating. See vet if symptoms last more than a few days or appetite is lost.

3. Chronic Kidney Disease

What it is: Very common in older cats. Kidneys gradually lose function.

Symptoms: Increased thirst and urination, weight loss, poor appetite, bad breath, lethargy.

What to do: Early blood tests help. Special kidney diets and treatment can slow progression.

4. Dental Disease

What it is: Extremely common in adult cats.

Symptoms: Bad breath, drooling, difficulty eating, red gums, pawing at mouth.

What to do: Regular dental checks and professional cleaning when needed.

5. Hyperthyroidism

What it is: Overactive thyroid gland, common in older cats.

Symptoms: Weight loss despite increased appetite, hyperactivity, vomiting, increased thirst.

What to do: Blood test confirms diagnosis. Medication, diet, or other treatments are available.

🐰 Common Illnesses in Rabbits

1. Gastrointestinal (GI) Stasis

What it is: The digestive system slows or stops. Very dangerous.

Symptoms: Small or no droppings, loss of appetite, hunched posture, lethargy.

What to do: Emergency. Take to a rabbit-savvy vet immediately. Prevention: unlimited hay and plenty of fiber.

2. Dental Problems (Overgrown Teeth)

What it is: Rabbit teeth grow continuously. Improper wear causes pain.

Symptoms: Drooling, difficulty eating, weight loss, preference for soft food.

What to do: Regular dental checks. Unlimited hay is the best prevention.

3. Snuffles (Pasteurellosis)

What it is: Bacterial respiratory infection.

Symptoms: Sneezing, runny nose, matted front paws, labored breathing.

What to do: Needs veterinary treatment with antibiotics. Keep environment clean and stress-free.

🐦 Common Illnesses in Birds

1. Respiratory Infections

What it is: Bacterial, viral, or fungal infections of the airways.

Symptoms: Tail bobbing, open-mouth breathing, discharge from nostrils, sneezing, lethargy.

What to do: Birds hide illness well. See an avian vet quickly if breathing changes.

2. Egg Binding (Female Birds)

What it is: Egg gets stuck inside the bird.

Symptoms: Straining, sitting at bottom of cage, swollen abdomen, loss of appetite.

What to do: Emergency. Keep warm and take to an avian vet immediately.

3. Psittacosis (Parrot Fever)

What it is: Bacterial disease that can also affect humans.

Symptoms: Fluffed feathers, lethargy, green droppings, discharge from eyes/nose, breathing difficulty.

What to do: Requires veterinary diagnosis and treatment. Good hygiene is important.

🚨 When to See a Vet Immediately

Seek emergency care if you notice:

• Not eating for more than 24 hours (especially cats and rabbits)
• Repeated vomiting or diarrhea with blood
• Difficulty breathing or blue/pale gums
• Collapse, seizures, or extreme lethargy
• Inability to urinate
• Sudden swelling of face or body
• Known poisoning

Important Health Tips

1

Always provide fresh water and change it daily.

2

Never give human medicines to pets — many are toxic.

3

Watch for sudden changes in appetite, energy, thirst, or bathroom habits.

4

Keep toxic foods away: chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, xylitol, avocado (for birds).

5

Keep a basic pet first-aid kit and know the nearest 24-hour emergency clinic.

6

Regular vet check-ups can catch problems early.
